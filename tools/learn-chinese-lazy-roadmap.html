    About this lazy Chinese roadmap

    Most beginner courses in Mandarin front-load the writing system, and most beginners quit somewhere inside it. This page inverts that order on purpose. Spoken Mandarin and written Chinese behave almost like two separate subjects, because a character carries very little information about how it sounds. There is no sounding out, so every character is a fresh memorisation task with no transfer to the next one. Speech has the opposite shape: the grammar is unusually forgiving and a small vocabulary goes a long way, which means useful conversation arrives far sooner than literacy does.

    Why speaking comes first here

    • The grammar is light. Verbs never conjugate, nouns have no plural ending, there is no grammatical gender, no articles, and no tense endings to memorise. Word order and a handful of small particles do the work instead.
    • Patterns are reusable forever. Because words never change shape, a sentence frame learned on day two is still correct on day two hundred with any noun dropped into it.
    • Handwriting has quietly become optional. Typing Mandarin means typing pinyin and choosing from a list, so recognition is the skill that matters in daily life, not stroke production.
    • Reading can wait, with one exception. Signage is the practical gap, so forty items are kept for recognition and nothing more.

    How the batches are sized

    Each step is deliberately small enough to finish, because an unfinished plan teaches nothing. Ten words cover a complete purchase, which means a genuine success on the first afternoon rather than three weeks of groundwork. Fifty words plus twelve frames reach ordering food and asking directions. One hundred handle transport, lodging and polite small talk. Two hundred support short conversations where you understand the reply. Past that point, talking to people teaches faster than drilling does, which is exactly where a lazy plan should hand over.

    How the daily session stays short

    A session is two new items and five reviews, scheduled at intervals of one, two, four, eight and sixteen days. The cap is deliberate: a deck that grows whenever you miss a day is the single most common reason people abandon spaced repetition. Here the deck cannot grow, there is no streak to protect, and a lazy button trims the session to three cards when that is all you have in you. Resuming after a gap simply brings back the items you were weakest on.

    Where the audio comes from

    Every item has a speak button driven by the Mandarin voice installed on your own device, at a slow teaching pace and at natural speed. Nothing is streamed from a server, so there are no recordings to download and the audio keeps working with no connection, provided the selected voice is a local system voice. The picker marks which of your voices run locally. Each phrase also carries a pitch contour, because seeing the shape of a tone while hearing it is what makes the distinction click for speakers of a language that uses pitch for feeling rather than for meaning.

    Questions people ask before starting Mandarin

    How many Chinese words do I need to start speaking?

    Far fewer than most courses imply. Ten are enough to complete a purchase: a pronoun, a verb meaning want, a word for this one, a way to ask the price, and a thank you. Fifty plus a dozen patterns cover meals and directions. Two hundred carry short conversations. Each step here ends in a capability rather than a percentage.

    Can I learn to speak without learning characters?

    Yes, and for a beginner it is quicker. Pinyin maps sound to spelling in roughly half an hour, doing the job an alphabet does elsewhere. Characters are switched off by default on this page and available on a single toggle whenever you decide you want them.

    What are the four tones, and what happens if I get one wrong?

    Pitch distinguishes meaning in Mandarin. One tone is high and level, another rises, another dips then rises, another falls sharply, and a light fifth is short and unstressed. The usual demonstration is the syllable ma, which shifts between mother, numb, horse and scold. A wrong tone normally produces a puzzled face rather than a problem, and context rescues you more often than beginners expect.

    Do I need a tutor alongside this?

    Not for the steps on this page, which are built to be self-checking through audio and recognition drills. A speaking partner becomes valuable around the two hundred word mark, when replies start arriving faster than you can decode them. That is the natural moment to add a human.

    Is Mandarin harder than Spanish for an English speaker?

    It divides neatly. Writing is harder, because literacy requires thousands of characters instead of a small alphabet. Grammar is easier, with no conjugation, no gender and no tense endings. The pronunciation sits in between, with a few consonants and the tone system needing deliberate attention early. That split is precisely what a speech-first ladder exploits.

    Why stop at around two hundred words?

    Because finishing is the point. Two hundred drilled words plus twelve frames reach the level where real use repays more than drilling, and a plan that ends is worth more than a longer one that gets abandoned. A final short step adds twenty words for thanking people and saying how you feel, which carry far more social weight than their number suggests. After that the next words arrive on their own, out of the conversations you are already managing to have.
